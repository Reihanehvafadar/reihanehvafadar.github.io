Research

PDEs, stochastic analysis,
and mathematical finance.

I study how singular coefficients and nonlinear interactions affect the existence, regularity, and quantitative behaviour of solutions.

01 · Analysis & probability

Singular diffusions
and regularity

My doctoral research at Université Laval focused on partial and stochastic differential equations with singular or distributional drifts. I study conditions under which these equations have well-defined solutions and investigate their regularity.

In joint work with Damir Kinzebulatov, I have studied elliptic regularity for divergence-free drifts and weak well-posedness for stochastic differential equations with critical distributional drifts. The methods connect energy estimates and semigroup theory with probabilistic representations.

02 · Stochastic control

Investment with
changing preferences

At McMaster, my work with Traian A. Pîrvu studies investment problems in which an investor’s risk preferences may change after a random event. Even when the market coefficients remain fixed, uncertainty about future preferences changes the optimization problem.

Our research develops the associated Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation and its nonlinear dual formulation. It connects existence and stochastic verification with scaling, elasticity, and curvature estimates that describe the value function and constrain the optimal investment policy.

03 · Quantitative analysis

Small-intensity
asymptotics

In a recent working manuscript with Traian A. Pîrvu, we examine the regime in which a change of preferences is unlikely. We obtain an explicit first-order expansion of the pre-switch value function, with a quadratic remainder uniform in time and on compact intervals of positive wealth.

The analysis combines weighted semigroup estimates for the dual equation with localization in the inverse Legendre transform. This gives a quantitative description of how rare preference changes affect the investor’s value function.

Looking ahead

Future research interests

I am interested in extending the connection between singular diffusion theory and nonlinear control equations. Directions I would like to develop include:

  • Controlled diffusions with singular drifts, with estimates that remain stable under regularization and optimization.
  • Preference dynamics with transitions in both directions, leading to coupled nonlinear control equations.
  • Stronger derivative estimates and error bounds for approximate investment strategies.
